# Where Tripoli's motor tests are fired, and whether its curves are corrected to sea level

This note asks where Tripoli Motor Testing (TMT) fires the motors it certifies, at what air pressure, and whether its
thrust curves are corrected to sea level as NFPA 1125 asks. It builds on [motor data](motor-data.md) §7 (no TRA letter
states its site), [nozzle exits](nozzle-exits.md) §3 (the exit areas) and [apogee bias](apogee-bias.md) §1. The sites
come from Tripoli's own news and the TMT chair's forum posts; elevations are USGS town-center points, not the stands
themselves. Pressure-thrust sizes are **Inference**: this note's arithmetic in the ISA. Sources read 2026-10-15.

**In short.** TMT has had no fixed site since 2017: it fires near where its chair lives, and that has moved.
- **North Carolina, firings from April 2017 (31 of the 49 TRA letters, dated 2018–2024).** The chair fired small
  motors at home in Carrboro (141 m), I–K on a farm in the same county, the largest and "sparky" ones on a gravel lot
  about two hours away [T6]. The named places sit at 99.6 kPa, 1.7–1.8 kPa below sea level (§2).
- **From 2025, the Denver area (4 letters so far).** Tripoli began moving the equipment there in 2024 [T3, T9]; the
  letterhead is Windsor, CO (1,462 m, 85.0 kPa). No document names the stand's spot (§3).
- **1998–2016 (14 letters, dated 1998–2014), and most of ThrustCurve's certification curves: not found.** 213 of
  the 237 TRA-certified solid motors with a certification-sourced curve got it before August 2020 (§4, §7).
- **No public TMT text corrects to sea level, or says how.** The 2020 procedures never mention pressure, altitude or
  sea level and defer to NFPA 1125 [T1]; the chair described results as measured "in our hands" [T7]. NFPA's text
  gives no method; NAR's manual says none was documented in 2011 [T13, T14] (§5).
- **Size.** Uncorrected, a Windsor–Denver curve reads +0.70% to +3.53% of average thrust high at sea level (16
  AeroTech reloads), about +0.4% to +4.6% of apogee, up to three times [apogee bias](apogee-bias.md) §1's 0.3–1.5%;
  at a 1,300 m field, +0.07% to +0.61%. A North Carolina curve there reads 0.55–2.59% low, like a sea-level one (§6).

## 2. 2017–2024: North Carolina

The chair from early 2017 until 2024 [T3] described the split himself [T6, post 14]. His home in Carrboro, NC took
nothing above a G or a quiet H. For very large motors and all sparky ones, a club member offered a construction
company's gravel lot about two hours away. New I–K motors were fired on a friend's farm in Orange County, NC.

The named towns sit at 141 m (Carrboro) and 149 m (Chapel Hill), 99.6 kPa in the ISA, 1.7–1.8 kPa below sea level
[T17]. The lot is not named. **Inference, illustrative only** (no drive-time source): two hours from Carrboro
reaches from the coast (Wilmington, 11 m) to the western Piedmont (Hickory, 348 m), 0.1–4.1 kPa below sea level;
higher ground lies a little farther. The procedures let the chair name several sites [T1, §3.2, p. 3].

## 3. From 2025: the Denver area

In September 2024 Tripoli's president wrote that the test equipment would leave the late chair's home in Chapel
Hill for "the Denver area" and the new chair [T3]; a forum member reported on 2024-09-15 that the move had begun
[T9]. TMT reopened in November 2025 with an updated in-house system [T4]; its first batch certified four new
AeroTech motors, with Quest nozzle changes [T5]. Their letters (tested 2025-10-06) carry a Windsor, CO letterhead.

No document read names the stand's site. Windsor is at 1,462 m (85.0 kPa), Denver at 1,596 m (83.6 kPa) [T17].
**Inference:** on the plains around them, Greeley (1,425 m) to Castle Rock (1,895 m) bound it at 80.5–85.3 kPa,
16.0–20.8 kPa below sea level: every TMT curve from 2025 on is a 1,400–1,900 m curve unless corrected.

## 4. Before 2017: not found

Searched without finding a site for 1998–2016: the 14 letters; Tripoli's TMT page, news (2023–2026) and TMT
announcements archived 2015–2021 [T2, T18]; 19 Rocketry Forum threads (2009–2026, cached); a 2010 AeroTech release;
web searches on each chair's name with "test stand" or "test site". The members-only HPR magazine archive was not
read (a forum member recalls an issue showing the first TMT session, "I think it was Jan/Feb 1992?"). The 2020
procedures allow a maker's or other third-party equipment approved for a session [T1, §6.2.1]; whether earlier
firings used it is unknown. The letterheads span 138–1,345 m and bound nothing.

## 5. Whether anyone corrects, and how

**NFPA 1125 asks for it, but gives no method in the text read.** Its high-power clause (A2021 cycle) starts "Static
testing, conducted at or corrected to sea level and 20°C ± 5°C (68°F ± 9°F)" [T13, FR-8, 8.2.7(1), p. 7]. The
2019 public inputs and ThrustCurve's paraphrase of the 2001 edition say the same [T13, PI-4; T12]; the A2025
cycle's four first revisions leave 8.2.7 alone [T13]. The paywalled full code and annexes were not read.

**TMT's procedures say nothing on it.** The 2020 policies (14 pages) contain no "sea level", "altitude",
"elevation", "barometric", "ambient" or "pressure", and name no conditioning temperature; casing temperature is
the only temperature they measure [T1, §4.13]. They "supplement NFPA 1125", which governs where they conflict
[T1, §2.4, p. 2]. Data reduction averages the valid firings and picks a typical curve, with no adjustment step
[T1, §8, p. 10]. The current version needs Tripoli's login [T2].

**What TMT's chair and a member wrote points to no correction (Inference).** The chair wrote in 2020 that makers
test "at altitudes, ambient temperatures, and relative humidities VERY different from those at my location", and
that TMT reports how motors performed in its hands [T7, post 1]. The member who posted the 2018 certifications and
their motor files said, offhand, that the pressure difference between the test site and AeroTech's factory could
alone explain a TMT-to-AeroTech gap [T8, post 19]. Had both corrected to sea level, elevation could not explain one.

**NAR keeps its sites low instead.** Its manual requires sites within 500 ft of sea level "until and unless"
a correction procedure is documented [T14, §1.5, p. 4]. The Estes L2350 sheet (2021) says only "Tested at 5276 ft.
MSL, Performance Corrected to Sea Level", with no method [T15, remarks].

**CAR allows a correction case by case.** Its chair may waive an impulse-class discrepancy explained by "test site
elevation or temperature conditioning", adjusting the data "to standard test conditions when possible" or noting
the cause [T16, §7.6.3, p. 14]. Sites are set by need [T16, §2.4]; where it fired Cesaroni's motors was not found.

**Temperature is a second, unsized cause.** TMT's procedures omit NFPA's 20 °C ± 5 °C conditioning; a warm grain
burns faster, nearly the same impulse (**Inference**).

## 6. What the uncorrected pressure term is worth

A rocket nozzle's thrust is its momentum thrust plus $(p_e - p_a) A_e$, with $p_e$ the exit pressure, $p_a$ the
outside pressure and $A_e$ the exit area. While the flow fills the nozzle, changing $p_a$ moves only the last
term. So a curve fired at site pressure $p_s$ and flown where the air is at $p_L$ is off by

$$
\Delta F = (p_L - p_s)\, A_e .
$$

Over the burn the impulse share equals $\Delta F/\bar F$, the share of average thrust: it scales with exit area,
not total impulse. Positive below means the curve gives more thrust than the motor makes at that field, so a
simulator that flies the curve as given (HPR Sim does, [motor model](../physics/motor.md)) puts apogee high.

The table scales [nozzle exits](nozzle-exits.md) §3's rounded shares at 14.67 kPa (L1256WS, 1.875 in exit, 2.08%;
its 16 reloads 0.63–2.92%) to fields at 0 m (101.3 kPa) and 1,300 m (86.7 kPa). The N3300R is 0.35 times the L1256WS.

lowers $p_L$ further and adds to every row; it is not bounded here.

- **The sign for TRA-certified motors depends on the year.** A North Carolina curve behaves like a sea-level one:
  flown at a 1,300 m field it reads low (the simulator's apogee low). A 2025-on curve, uncorrected, reads high at
  sea level and nearly right at a field near 1,300 m. For 1998–2016 curves the sign is unbounded between the two.

## 7. Which ThrustCurve curves are certification data

ThrustCurve tags each file's source: a certification organization's page, the maker's, or user-made [T11]. Of the
466 TRA-certified solid motors its API returned, 237 have a certification-sourced file (AeroTech 143, Loki 37,
Cesaroni 20, Gorilla 12, Quest 9, Ellis 8, AMW 4, Kosdon 2, KBA 2), 92 only maker files, 85 only user files and 52
none (counted over every file) [T11]. The tag doesn't say which certifier: some, Cesaroni's above all, may be CAR's.

File ids encode their upload time. 213 of the 237 motors have a certification file uploaded before ThrustCurve's
migration of 2020-08-23; 29 files came later (2020: 4, 2021: 8, 2022: 4, 2023: 4, 2024: 6, 2025: 3) [T11]. TMT's
list and letters name 24 motors certified from February 2018 to September 2020, 17 of them among the 213 [T10, T18].
**Inference:** most of the other 196 hold data from before 2017, from sites not found (§4); a few may be 2017
North Carolina firings or CAR data. Maker files have their own site: AeroTech's factory [T8], RCS in Cedar City, UT
on letters from 2006 on (Las Vegas, NV in 2001) [T10]. Whether AeroTech corrects is not published.

## What this suggests

- **Don't assume one sign for "TRA certified".** A thrust-at-altitude term ([ideas](ideas-design-and-physics.md))
  could take each curve's test pressure as an input: 99.6 kPa for TMT firings of 2017–2024, 83.6–85.0 kPa from
  2025, and unknown (flagged, sea level assumed) otherwise. Expected effect: at a 1,300 m field, NC-era and NAR
  curves gain 0.55–2.92% of thrust; 2025-on curves change by under 0.61%.
- **Motor data §7 can widen** its uncorrected-curve line (+1.13% and +0.58% of thrust from 1,500 m) to +0.70–3.53%
  at sea level for a Windsor–Denver curve (drawn exits), and say where TMT fired by era (§1–3); its count of 0 of 49
  letters stating a site stands.
- **Data use.** Tripoli's pages are "all rights reserved"; the 2020 procedures and forum posts carry no license;
  NFPA's reports are copyrighted: cite, quote briefly, cache under `refs/` only. USGS heights are public domain.

## Open questions

- Where TMT fired from 1992 to 2016 and fires now, and whether its 2025 system or members-only procedures correct
  to sea level or record site pressure. The committee (tmt@tripoli.org) or the HPR archive could answer.
- How Estes and NAR corrected the L2350, and whether AeroTech and Loki correct their own maker curves.
- Whether every hobby nozzle flows full at sea level (separation breaks the $(p_L - p_s) A_e$ rule), and how much
  an outdoor stand's temperature moves thrust and burn time.
