# Hagerty's 1975 paper: no complete copy found, but Estes's parts pin the fin stations

This note follows [hagerty-tunnel-lift](hagerty-tunnel-lift.md), which read the NARAM-52 scan of Hagerty's tunnel
report on an Estes Cineroc/Omega [H]. That scan lacks printed pp. 8, 9 and 11, Fig. 1 (the configuration drawing) and
Eq. 8. This note looked for a complete copy, then pinned what it could of the missing geometry from Estes's own parts
lists and catalogs. **Inference** marks this note's own arithmetic.

**In short: no complete copy turned up in nine catalogs and indexes searched on 2026-11-04; the author's own copy
and the NAR's original competition entry are the likeliest places left.** Estes's parts do pin the stage split. The
booster is a 5.00 in (127 mm) BT-60R tube and the upper stage a 14.00 in (356 mm) BT-60AD tube, with each fin set's
trailing edge flush with its stage's aft end. The upper fins' trailing edge therefore sits 5.0 in ahead of the booster
fins'. That is the spacing the earlier note assumed for its high end, and it rules out the 3.5 in that gave its low
end. The center-of-pressure (CP) shift that issue
[#730 (a fin set behind another)](https://github.com/nrdptel/fusionspace-eridanus/issues/730) quotes narrows from
0.8–2.0 to **1.0–2.0 calibers** (1.4–2.0 near 0°). Confidence: high in the tube lengths, which Estes's 1974
parts catalog and a listing checked by measurement agree on, and a catalog length checks to 0.8%. Confidence is
medium that Hagerty's model used the stock booster: his own freehand sketch draws it 3.9–4.2 in long. The α sweeps'
speed, the tail fairing, Eq. 8 and configurations 2 and 3 stay unknown.

## 2. The stage split from Estes's parts

The Omega's airframe is fixed by its tube part numbers, and Estes's 1974 parts catalog gives their lengths.

- **The tubes.** The K-52P instructions list a BT-60AD upper-stage tube, a BT-60R booster tube and a BT-60R payload
  tube ([E] sheet a, parts list; sheet c, steps 7 and 11). Estes's 1974 Custom Parts Catalog, as transcribed on
  Ninfinger, gives the BT-60AD as 14" and the BT-60R as 5", both 1.637 in outside diameter [P74]. Brohm's 2005 kit
  reference gives the Omega the same tubes at 14.00 in and 5.00 in (PDF p. 8); it says its lengths were checked by
  measuring tubes where possible (PDF p. 4) [B05]. A 1995 table by J. Garza, with J. Brohm, agrees [T95].
- **The fins.** Both sets' trailing edges are flush with their stage's aft end ([E] sheet f, step 16; sheet e,
  step 17). The stages butt together; the JT-60C coupler, 1.5 in long [P74], sits inside them ([E] sheet c, step 10).
- **A check on the lengths (Inference).** The 1975 catalog gives the Omega with payload section and nose as 30.5 in
  (77.5 cm) long [C75, p. 17]. Booster, upper stage and payload tube (5 + 14 + 5 in) plus the 6.75 in PNC-60AH nose
  [P74] make 30.75 in, 0.8% longer.
- **The Cineroc.** Estes's 1974 and 1975 catalogs give it as 9.9 in (25.15 cm) long and 1.75 in (44.5 mm) across,
  fitting a BT-60 tube [C74, p. 49; C75, p. 34]. A builder quotes the same two figures from Stine's *Handbook of
  Model Rocketry* [S]. **Inference:** Hagerty's 28 in less the two stages' 19.0 in leaves 9.0 in of Cineroc in view.
  About 0.9 in would then sit inside the tube, or the 28 in drops 0.9 in.
- **Which kit.** The instructions read are the K-52P's (code 83080A). The 1975 catalog sells one Omega for both uses,
  with the payload section "for use without Cineroc" [C75, p. 17], so the camera rode on the same two stages.

**Hagerty's own sketch.** Fig. 8's configuration-1a sketch (PDF p. 20) shows a stage joint at the fins' trailing edge
and a finless booster behind it. This note measured it along the drawn axis, scaled to the 28 in dimension line.
The joint sits 3.9–4.2 in from the base. The camera's full diameter runs from about 2.7 to 7.3 in from the tip,
and its taper ends near 8.8 in, close to the 9.0 in derived above. The sketch is freehand: its fin roots are
1.8–2.1 in against the 3 in written beside them. It agrees with a booster of a few inches but can't tell 4 in from 5.

**Stations from the nose tip (Inference),** on the report's fin plans [H] and the kit's stages, 28 in overall:

| Part | From the tip |
|---|--:|
| Cineroc, in view | 0 to 9.0 in |
| Upper stage (BT-60AD) | 9.0 to 23.0 in |
| Upper fins, root leading to trailing edge | 20.0 to 23.0 in |
| Booster (BT-60R) | 23.0 to 28.0 in |
| Booster fins, root leading to trailing edge | 24.5 to 28.0 in |

The open gap from the upper fins' trailing edge to the booster fins' leading edge is 1.5 in, 0.92 calibers of the
1.625 in body. On the kit's fin pattern (booster root 3.69 in) it is 1.31 in, 0.81 calibers.
[Tandem-fin-downwash](tandem-fin-downwash.md) lists this gap as 0–1 caliber, assumed. Configuration 2, the upper
stage alone, would be 23.0 in long plus a tail fairing whose size isn't given.

## 3. What the stations do to #730's CP shift (Inference)

Summing both fin sets puts the CP at x̄; if the booster set keeps only a share η of its own slope, the CP moves
forward by

  Δx = (1 − η) C_b (x_b − x̄) / (S − (1 − η) C_b),

where S is the summed normal-force slope and C_b and x_b are the booster set's slope and CP.

The inputs are those of [hagerty-tunnel-lift](hagerty-tunnel-lift.md#5-two-fin-sets-in-line), on 15.5 cm² and
calibers of 1.75 in. The nose gives 2.0 per rad at 2 in, the shoulder −0.28 at 7 in. The upper fins give 14.27 at
20.87 in (Barrowman's CP, 0.87 in behind the root's leading edge) and the booster fins 20.59 at 25.49 in. So S is
36.58 per rad and x̄ is 22.55 in, 2.95 in ahead of x_b. At η = 0.04, Δx = 0.96 × 20.59 × 2.95 / (36.58 − 19.77) =
3.47 in, 1.98 calibers.

| Booster share η | Basis (the earlier note's section 5) | 5.0 in (kit) | 4.0 in | 3.5 in |
|--:|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 0.01 | low end of the near-0° readings with their error | 2.12 | 1.84 | 1.70 |
| 0.04 | near 0°, low | 1.98 | 1.72 | 1.59 |
| 0.19 | near 0°, high | 1.41 | 1.22 | 1.13 |
| 0.28 | high end with its error | 1.15 | 1.00 | 0.92 |
| 0.33 | secant at 23° | 1.02 | 0.88 | 0.82 |

The columns give the CP shift in calibers for each trailing-edge spacing: the kit's, the sketch's and the earlier
low end.

- **Size.** With the kit's 5.0 in, the shift is 1.0–2.0 calibers over the measured shares, 1.4–2.0 near 0°. On the
  1.625 in body it is 8% larger in calibers (1.1–2.1). The sign is unchanged: summing reads the margin high.
- **What else moves it.** The kit's fin pattern instead of the report's moves each value by 0.05 caliber or less.
  The Cineroc fully out (28.9 in overall) adds 0.03 or less. A nose CP from 1 to 2 in and a shoulder from 7 to 8.5 in
  move it 0.05 or less. Each inch of spacing moves it 0.13–0.28 caliber, so the sketch's 4 in would give 0.9–1.7.
- **What's left.** The range now rests on the measured share η, not the positions. The share's own uncertainty
  (±3–9 points near 0°) spans 2.1 down to 1.15 calibers.

## 4. What stays missing

- **Printed pp. 8, 9 and 11:** the results, conclusions and references, by the earlier note's reading of the pages.
- **Eq. 8,** which Fig. 8 cites for its slopes. The earlier note matched them to the report's Eq. 6 to three digits.
- **The α sweeps' speed.** The earlier note's inference (about 9 m/s, from the crossflow Reynolds-number cap) stands.
- **The tail fairing's** shape and length, and whether the 28 in includes it.
- **Configuration 3's** casing: whether the spent motor stands proud of the upper stage, and by how much.
- **The sting's station.** Fig. 3's photo (PDF p. 13) appears to show it entering near the Cineroc's aft end. The
  report measured no moment, so this matters only for a tare.

## What the project can use

- **Terms.** [H] is a NAR member benefit with no license: cite it, cache it privately, commit nothing from it. The
  Estes instructions, catalogs and parts catalog are Estes's, scanned or transcribed by hobby sites "for reference use
  only" [P74]: cite them; the page images are cached privately under `refs/research/`. Brohm's reference [B05] and
  Garza's table [T95] state no license: cite only. Tube lengths are facts and are restated here, not copied.
- **For #730.** Take 5.0 in between the sets' trailing edges as sourced, not assumed, and quote the CP shift as
  1.0–2.0 calibers (1.4–2.0 near 0°). The test that pins the summed margin's sign can use these stations. Expected
  effect: the issue's low end rises by 0.2 caliber; the sign and high end don't change.
- **For [#731 (flying configuration 1a)](https://github.com/nrdptel/fusionspace-eridanus/issues/731).** The geometry
  gains the stage lengths (5.0 and 14.0 in) and the Cineroc's 9.9 in length. The nose shape and the camera's
  diameter change stay as the earlier note assumed. Normal force near 0° doesn't depend on them.
- **For tandem-fin-downwash.** Hagerty's gap becomes 0.92 calibers (0.81 on the kit's fins), sourced. Its
  NACA 1307 vortex estimate could be rerun at that gap.

## Open questions

- **The author's or the NAR's original.** Either would settle every item in section 4. Neither is reachable from
  public catalogs.
- **Did Hagerty fly the stock booster?** His sketch draws it about a fifth short, but it also draws the fins a third
  short. Fig. 1 would tell.
- **The *Astronautics & Aeronautics* lead.** Whether vol. 13 (1975) names the conference's winners, and whether that
  points to a proceedings volume.
