# ADR-195: Neer's 2026-10-07 guides: rocketry explained, illustrated and live (2026-10-07)

- **Status:** accepted; amends [ADR-162, the suite and its releases][adr-162] §3 (the queue), and [ADR-147, a roadmap of open work][adr-147] §6 (its budget)
- **Summary:** Neer's guides to all of high-power rocketry become M0.7, *Rocketry explained*, a section of the existing docs site in four increments. M0.7a, right after release 0.1, sets the format and writes four guides on the largest gaps that hpr already models: stability, fin flutter, drag through Mach 1, and how far to trust a simulation. M0.7b, before release 0.4, adds recovery, wind and rail exit, and motors. M0.7c, after release 0.5, makes the figures live, run by hpr itself in the browser. M0.7d, after 1.0, covers the rest of the hobby, mostly by linking out. Every number a guide shows comes from hpr or a cited source, every figure is checked for clipping, and nothing is copied from copyrighted or share-alike sources. The roadmap's budget rises to 38,500 bytes.

**Context.** On October 7, 2026, the project took on in-depth guides, with strong visuals and
possibly interaction, to everything in high-power rocketry ([VISION](../VISION.md), that date).

The repository has about 62 site pages. Most are reference; the hobbyist pages are a tutorial,
task pages such as *Fly your .ork* and *Pick a motor*, and one explanation (*How a flight is
simulated*). The site has one hand-drawn
diagram, one generated plot and no interactive figure. No milestone plans guides; the nearest
are M6.8's launch-day guide, M9.2's 3D replay and a "classroom mode" in the ideas backlog.
Outside, the research ([the guides note](../research/guides.md)) found deep books, a magazine
archive and many one-formula calculators, but no modern guide that draws the physics and lets a
reader change it. NASA Glenn's pages come nearest, at school level and subsonic. The largest gaps
are topics hpr already models: stability as Mach and angle of attack move the center of pressure,
fin flutter (the forms most often copied overstate the flutter speed by √2), drag through Mach 1,
and how far to trust a simulation.

**Decision.**

1. **The guides are a section of the docs site, *Rocketry explained*, not a new product.** They
   are explanation pages in [the writing guide](../writing.md)'s sense. The site already has
   how-to guides, so the section's name keeps the two apart. Docs are a deliverable from Neer's
   brief (V15), and the site is the one place a reader finds answers (north star 6).
2. **M0.7a, right after release 0.1:** the format and four guides, on stability, fin flutter,
   drag and Mach, and how far to trust a simulation. They need only physics that 0.1 ships and
   reports that are committed, and 0.1 is when outside readers first arrive. This puts the
   flight analyzer (M7.1) one increment later; the guides are smaller, and the brief asked for
   both.
3. **M0.7b, before release 0.4:** recovery as a timeline (dual deploy, descent, drift and charges,
   after M6.7 and M6.8), wind with weathercocking and rail exit, and motors with thrust curves and
   delays.
4. **M0.7c, after release 0.5:** the figures become live. A reader changes a number in the text
   (fin thickness, wind speed, a motor) and the figure redraws from `hpr-wasm` in the browser,
   with no server. It waits for 0.5 so that it reuses the app's plotting code rather than building
   a second set, and it counts as one of the three user-facing surfaces in progress.
5. **M0.7d, after release 1.0:** the rest of the hobby: certification, the safety codes and
   waivers, construction, avionics wiring, staging and air starts. These are covered well
   elsewhere, so the pages link to the official and best sources more than they write. Rule pages
   carry a source date, a stale-after date that CI fails past, and "not legal advice". Each line after 1.0 (motors,
   parachutes, avionics) adds its own guide in its own milestone.
6. **Every guide follows one format,** checked in the milestones' *done when*:
   - it opens with what it covers, who it is for and how far to trust it;
   - words come before equations, then a worked example whose numbers a program run in CI prints;
   - every number in the prose is regenerated by CI or read from a committed report by a test;
   - each figure shows one idea, drawn in the product system's drawing language, and is generated
     from hpr's output by a committed command that CI reruns, so a stale figure fails;
   - uncertainty is drawn as a band, and the flattering side is marked, tied to the model's test
     of which way it errs;
   - Neer's no-clipping rule is checked, not eyeballed: a figure's text or marks leaving its
     frame fails the build, and so does a page that scrolls sideways at any width from 320 to
     1,920 px; live figures are checked at each input's extremes.
7. **Guides carry no verdicts and copy nothing.** No page says a rocket is safe or will pass; the
   RSO and the safety code decide. Charge sizes appear only as M6.7's ranges, after "ground test
   first". NFPA 1127, the exam pools, the magazines and OpenRocket's CC BY-SA docs are cited and
   linked, never copied; exam questions are never answered. NASA's public-domain figures may be
   reused with credit.
8. **The roadmap's budget rises** from 36,000 to 38,500 bytes, for M0.7's entry and four queue
   lines.

**Alternatives considered.**

- **A separate guides site or app.** Rejected: it would split the reader's search, and the docs
  site already has the build, the link checks and the product system.
- **Live figures from the start.** Rejected: they need `hpr-wasm` (M4.4), and before 0.5 they
  would bring a second plotting stack. Static figures generated from hpr come first and stay as
  the page's fallback with scripts off.
- **All the topics at once, before the analyzer.** Rejected: certification, construction and
  wiring are covered well elsewhere, and recovery's guide needs M6.7's ranges first.
- **After release 1.0, like the new product lines.** Rejected: guides are documentation, part of
  every release under the brief, and the four gap topics draw on physics already shipped.

**Consequences.**

- The flight analyzer starts one increment later.
- Each guide's numbers move with the models: a figure or worked example that changes with a model
  change shows up in CI, the same as any other docs code.
- [M4.4][m4-4]'s WebAssembly package gets its first reader-facing user in M0.7c.
- "A classroom mode", in the ideas backlog, can build on the guides later; it stays there.
